Version: 1.0

Status: Official

Last Updated: August 2026

# Purpose

The Learning Journey is the central learning path inside HanGo.

Its purpose is to guide learners from intermediate Chinese proficiency toward advanced communication skills through structured progression, realistic situations, and continuous practice.

The Learning Journey transforms language learning from isolated lessons into a complete educational experience.

---

# Core Concept

Traditional language learning often organizes content by:

- Vocabulary lists.
- Grammar chapters.
- Textbook units.
- Difficulty levels.

HanGo organizes learning by:

- Real-life situations.
- Communication goals.
- Competencies.
- Progressive challenges.

The learner does not simply study Chinese.

The learner uses Chinese to accomplish increasingly complex objectives.

---

# Learning Structure

The Learning Journey is organized into four main levels:

1. Scenario

2. Stage

3. Lesson

4. Activity

---

# 1. Scenario

A Scenario represents a real-life context where Chinese is needed.

Examples:

Daily Life:

- Restaurant.
- Shopping.
- Transportation.
- Hotel.
- Hospital.
- Making friends.

Professional:

- Job interview.
- Office communication.
- Meetings.
- Presentations.
- Negotiations.

Academic:

- University.
- Research.
- Presentations.
- Discussions.

Advanced Society:

- Economy.
- Technology.
- Culture.
- Politics.
- Current events.

Each Scenario has a communication purpose.

The learner should understand:

"Why am I learning this?"

---

# Scenario Difficulty Levels

Each Scenario contains multiple difficulty levels.

## Intermediate

Focus:

- Understanding common situations.
- Building confidence.
- Using essential structures.
- Maintaining short conversations.

Example:

Restaurant:

Ordering food.

Asking about prices.

Requesting changes.

---

## Upper Intermediate

Focus:

- More natural conversations.
- Longer responses.
- More complex grammar.
- Greater vocabulary range.

Example:

Restaurant:

Discussing preferences.

Explaining dietary restrictions.

Making recommendations.

---

## Advanced

Focus:

- Nuanced communication.
- Opinions.
- Cultural understanding.
- Professional and academic language.

Example:

Restaurant:

Discussing food culture.

Comparing regional cuisines.

Debating social trends.

# Lesson Structure

Every lesson follows a consistent structure.

A lesson contains:

1. Objective

2. Context Introduction

3. Vocabulary

4. Grammar

5. Dialogue

6. Listening Practice

7. Speaking Practice

8. Writing Practice

9. Exercises

10. Mission

11. Evaluation

---

# 1. Objective

Every lesson must define what the learner will be able to do.

Example:

"After this lesson, the learner can order a meal, ask questions about ingredients, and express preferences in Chinese."

Learning objectives must focus on communication abilities.

---

# 2. Context Introduction

Before learning vocabulary or grammar, learners understand the situation.

The learner should know:

- Who is speaking.
- Where the conversation happens.
- What the objective is.
- What problem needs to be solved.

---

# 3. Vocabulary

Vocabulary is introduced through context.

Each vocabulary item includes:

- Chinese characters.
- Pinyin.
- Meaning.
- Pronunciation.
- Example sentences.
- Related vocabulary.
- Previous connections.

Vocabulary should never exist as isolated information.

---

# 4. Grammar

Grammar is introduced as a communication tool.

Every grammar point includes:

- Meaning.
- When to use it.
- Sentence structure.
- Examples.
- Common mistakes.
- Comparison with similar structures.

The learner should understand why the grammar exists.

---

# 5. Dialogue

Dialogues represent realistic communication.

They should include:

- Natural expressions.
- Appropriate difficulty.
- Cultural context.
- Previously learned vocabulary.
- Target grammar structures.

Dialogues should not feel like artificial textbook conversations.

---

# 6. Listening Practice

Learners practice understanding spoken Chinese.

Activities include:

- Normal speed audio.
- Slower audio.
- Key phrase identification.
- Comprehension questions.

---

# 7. Speaking Practice

Learners actively produce Chinese.

Activities include:

- Repetition.
- Shadowing.
- Role-play.
- Free conversation.
- AI interaction.

---

# 8. Writing Practice

Learners produce written Chinese.

Activities include:

- Sentence creation.
- Short messages.
- Paragraphs.
- Longer compositions.

---

# 9. Exercises

Exercises reinforce learning.

Possible activities:

- Fill in the blanks.
- Sentence ordering.
- Translation.
- Listening comprehension.
- Grammar selection.
- Vocabulary application.

Exercises should always connect to communication.

---

# 10. Mission

Every lesson ends with a practical objective.

The learner must use Chinese to accomplish something.

Examples:

- Order food without help.
- Explain a problem.
- Ask for information.
- Write a message.
- Give an opinion.

Missions transform knowledge into ability.

---

# 11. Evaluation

The learner receives feedback.

Evaluation includes:

- Accuracy.
- Vocabulary usage.
- Grammar.
- Pronunciation.
- Fluency.
- Communication effectiveness.

The goal is improvement, not punishment.

---

# Progressive Difficulty

Difficulty increases through:

- More vocabulary.
- More complex grammar.
- Longer conversations.
- Faster speech.
- More cultural context.
- More abstract topics.
- Greater independence.

The learner should always feel challenged but capable.

# Completion Philosophy

Completing the Learning Journey does not mean finishing Chinese.

It means reaching a level where the learner can continue improving independently through real communication.
