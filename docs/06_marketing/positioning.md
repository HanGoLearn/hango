# Purpose

This document defines how HanGo should be positioned in the market.

Its purpose is to ensure that product communication, landing pages, launch messages, advertising, and future sales materials describe HanGo consistently.

Positioning should explain:

- Who HanGo is for.
- What problem HanGo solves.
- Why HanGo is different.
- What result the learner can expect.
- Why the product is worth using.

---

# Positioning Objective

HanGo should be positioned as a structured AI-powered Chinese learning platform focused on real communication.

It should not be presented as:

- A generic chatbot.
- A vocabulary memorization application.
- A simple pronunciation tool.
- A traditional textbook platform.
- An official HSK examination service.
- A quick-fluency solution.

HanGo combines structure, personalization, practice, and feedback inside one learning system.

---

# Target Audience

The initial target audience includes independent Chinese learners who:

- Already understand basic Chinese.
- Are approximately between HSK 3 and early HSK 5.
- Can recognize vocabulary and grammar but struggle to use Chinese actively.
- Want more speaking and writing practice.
- Need a clear learning path.
- Study without regular access to a private teacher.
- Want explanations in English or Spanish.
- Are comfortable using AI-supported learning tools.

---

# Primary Target Learner

The primary MVP learner is:

An intermediate Chinese learner who has studied vocabulary and grammar but still hesitates when speaking, writing, or handling real-life situations.

This learner usually experiences problems such as:

- Understanding lessons but not speaking confidently.
- Knowing words but not using them naturally.
- Depending too much on Pinyin or translation.
- Not knowing what to study next.
- Receiving generic AI answers without curriculum structure.
- Having little opportunity to practice realistic communication.
- Not understanding whether progress is meaningful.

---

# Initial Geographic Focus

HanGo can initially target Spanish-speaking and English-speaking learners.

A practical initial focus may include:

- Peru.
- Latin America.
- Spain.
- English-speaking international learners.

The MVP does not need different product versions by country.

Marketing messages may be localized while the core product remains the same.

# Learner Problem

Many intermediate learners reach a point where traditional study becomes insufficient.

They may have:

- Completed beginner courses.
- Learned hundreds or thousands of words.
- Studied grammar.
- Used flashcards.
- Watched videos.
- Practiced HSK exercises.

However, they still struggle to:

- Respond quickly.
- Maintain conversations.
- Write naturally.
- Understand real situations.
- Apply grammar without thinking excessively.
- Identify their actual weaknesses.
- Organize independent study.

The problem is not only lack of information.

The problem is lack of structured active use.

# Competitive Context

HanGo competes indirectly with several product categories.

---

## Language Learning Applications

Typical strengths:

- Structured curriculum.
- Easy onboarding.
- Gamification.
- Habit formation.

Typical limitations:

- Limited open communication.
- Repetitive exercises.
- Weak advanced speaking practice.
- Generic progress metrics.

HanGo differentiates through deeper production, realistic missions, and contextual AI support.

---

## AI Chatbots

Typical strengths:

- Flexible conversation.
- Immediate explanations.
- Broad topic coverage.

Typical limitations:

- No stable curriculum.
- Weak progress tracking.
- Inconsistent teaching behavior.
- Learner must decide what to study.
- Limited connection between explanation and long-term development.

HanGo differentiates through structure, educational rules, learner context, and competency tracking.

---

## Private Tutors

Typical strengths:

- Human interaction.
- Personalized correction.
- Flexible explanations.

Typical limitations:

- Higher cost.
- Limited availability.
- Inconsistent study frequency.
- Difficult progress continuity between sessions.

HanGo should not claim to replace every human teacher.

It provides continuous structured support that complements or partially substitutes private tutoring for independent practice.

---

## HSK Preparation Platforms

Typical strengths:

- Exam alignment.
- Vocabulary coverage.
- Practice questions.
- Mock examinations.

Typical limitations:

- Strong test focus.
- Limited real-life communication.
- Weak open speaking and writing practice.

HanGo may reference HSK levels, but its primary promise is practical communication.

---

## Flashcard and Vocabulary Tools

Typical strengths:

- Efficient memorization.
- Spaced repetition.
- Simple review.

Typical limitations:

- Weak contextual use.
- Limited speaking and writing.
- Vocabulary knowledge may remain passive.

HanGo positions vocabulary as part of communication rather than the final objective.

# Key Differentiators

## 1. Structured AI Learning

HanGo combines AI flexibility with an organized curriculum.

The learner receives both:

- A clear path.
- Personalized support.

---

## 2. Persistent Context-Aware AI Teacher

The AI Teacher understands:

- The current scenario.
- The current lesson.
- The selected dialogue line.
- The grammar being studied.
- The learner's recent answer.
- The learner's language preference.

The learner does not need to explain the context repeatedly.

---

## 3. Real-Life Scenario Learning

Learning is organized around situations such as:

- Restaurant.
- Hotel.
- Shopping.
- Travel.
- Work.
- Social interaction.

The objective is to complete real communication tasks.

---

## 4. Active Production

HanGo prioritizes:

- Speaking.
- Writing.
- Conversation.
- Missions.
- Practical responses.

The learner is not limited to selecting answers.

---

## 5. Competency-Based Progress

Progress reflects abilities such as:

- Ordering food independently.
- Explaining a problem.
- Maintaining a conversation.
- Writing a practical message.

HanGo does not measure progress only through points or lesson completion.

---

## 6. Integrated Learning System

Lessons, AI, speaking, writing, missions, review, and progress belong to one connected product.

The learner does not need multiple disconnected tools.

# Target User Objections

## “I can already use a general AI chatbot.”

Response direction:

A general chatbot can answer questions, but HanGo provides a structured curriculum, lesson context, speaking and writing activities, missions, review, and progress tracking.

---

## “I already use a language-learning app.”

Response direction:

HanGo is designed for learners who have completed beginner material but still need to turn passive knowledge into real communication.

---

## “I can study with YouTube or textbooks.”

Response direction:

Videos and textbooks provide information, but HanGo organizes practice, feedback, active production, and progress into one guided system.

---

## “AI corrections may be unreliable.”

Response direction:

HanGo uses controlled educational instructions, structured lesson context, focused feedback, and human-reviewed core content. AI feedback should remain transparent and practical.

---

## “I am not advanced enough.”

Response direction:

The MVP is designed primarily for intermediate learners. Support includes Pinyin, translations, hints, slower audio, and explanations in English or Spanish.

# Positioning Risks

## Risk 1 — Appearing Like a Generic Chatbot

Mitigation:

Lead with the structured Learning Journey and real-life scenarios.

---

## Risk 2 — Appearing Like Another Beginner App

Mitigation:

Clearly state the intermediate learner focus and active communication objective.

---

## Risk 3 — Excessive Feature Messaging

Mitigation:

Communicate one central outcome: using Chinese in real situations.

---

## Risk 4 — Overpromising AI Accuracy

Mitigation:

Describe AI as personalized educational support, not perfect judgment.

---

## Risk 5 — Confusing HSK Preparation With Core Positioning

Mitigation:

Use HSK as a proficiency reference, not as the primary product promise.

# Positioning Validation

Before public launch, test whether target learners understand:

- What HanGo is.
- Who it is for.
- How it differs from a chatbot.
- How it differs from a beginner app.
- What result they can expect.
- Why speaking and writing are central.
- Why the Learning Journey matters.

Validation methods may include:

- Interviews.
- Landing-page tests.
- Beta surveys.
- Message-comprehension tests.
- Registration conversion.
- User feedback.

---

# Positioning Success Criteria

Positioning is successful when target learners can explain HanGo as:

A structured Chinese learning platform that helps intermediate learners practice real communication with contextual AI support.

Positioning is weak when users describe HanGo only as:

- A Chinese chatbot.
- A flashcard app.
- A pronunciation tool.
- An HSK exercise app.
- Another Duolingo-style game.

---

# Official Positioning Summary

```text
HanGo is a structured AI-powered Chinese learning platform for intermediate learners.

It helps learners turn vocabulary and grammar knowledge into practical speaking and writing through real-life scenarios, contextual lessons, missions, personalized review, and a persistent AI Teacher.
```

# Success Definition

The HanGo market positioning succeeds when the right learners quickly understand the product, recognize their own problem in the message, and see HanGo as a credible path from passive knowledge to real Chinese communication.
