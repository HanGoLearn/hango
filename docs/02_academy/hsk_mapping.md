# Purpose

This document defines how HanGo aligns its curriculum with the HSK 3.0 proficiency framework while maintaining its communication-based learning methodology.

HanGo is not an HSK preparation platform. It is a Chinese communication platform that uses HSK as a standardized proficiency reference.

This approach ensures that learners can develop real-world communication skills while remaining compatible with internationally recognized HSK standards.

---

# Educational Philosophy

HSK defines **what learners should know**.

HanGo defines **what learners should be able to do**.

The curriculum is therefore organized around communication scenarios while ensuring that the vocabulary, grammar, and competencies expected by HSK are progressively covered.

---

# HanGo Progression

HanGo covers the full HSK 3.0 range, from an absolute beginner through
near-native fluency:

- HSK 1–2 (Foundations)
- HSK 3–4 (Everyday Life)
- HSK 5–6 (Advanced Situations, early)
- HSK 7–9 (Advanced Situations, late — reachable only through sustained
  Writing Lab / Speaking Lab practice beyond the guided curriculum)

The learner-facing Learning Journey groups this into 3 chapters —
Foundations, Everyday Life, Advanced Situations — not by HSK level
directly. See `points_system.md` for how General Level points map to a
named difficulty tier (not an HSK number) and how the 3 chapters relate to
the 9 content domains behind them.

---

# Relationship Between HanGo and HSK

HSK is used as a reference for:

- Vocabulary coverage.
- Grammar progression.
- Recommended difficulty.
- Reading complexity.
- Listening complexity.

HanGo does **not** organize lessons according to HSK textbook chapters.

Instead, HSK content is distributed naturally across communication scenarios.

# Curriculum Mapping

## HSK 1–2

Primary Objective

Build absolute-beginner survival Chinese from zero.

Typical scenarios include:

- Greetings and introductions.
- Numbers, time, and dates.
- Family.
- Weather and seasons.
- Daily routines and feelings.
- Around the house.
- Basic shopping.
- Phone and communication basics.
- School and classroom.
- Hobbies and free time.
- Simple health and pharmacy visits.
- Basic banking and money.
- Asking for and giving simple directions.

Learners produce short, simple sentences and handle predictable daily
exchanges. This tier (the Foundations domain) was added 2026-08-09 — it
did not exist in earlier versions of this document.

---

## HSK 3

Primary Objective

Develop confidence in everyday communication.

Typical scenarios include:

- Restaurant.
- Shopping.
- Transportation.
- Daily routines.
- Friends.
- Family.
- Weather.
- Health.
- School.
- Work.

Learners begin producing longer conversations and expressing personal experiences.

---

## HSK 4

Primary Objective

Handle more complex daily interactions.

Typical scenarios include:

- Traveling independently.
- Solving problems.
- Making appointments.
- Giving explanations.
- Expressing preferences.
- Comparing alternatives.
- Describing events.
- Discussing future plans.

Learners begin communicating with greater independence.

---

## HSK 5

Primary Objective

Participate comfortably in professional and academic conversations.

Typical scenarios include:

- Workplace communication.
- Meetings.
- Presentations.
- Interviews.
- Business travel.
- Customer service.
- News discussions.
- Cultural topics.

Learners become capable of sustaining extended conversations.

---

## HSK 6

Primary Objective

Communicate fluently across a wide range of situations.

Typical scenarios include:

- Negotiations.
- Problem solving.
- Formal discussions.
- Public speaking.
- Professional writing.
- Abstract topics.
- Current events.

Learners should communicate with confidence and flexibility.

---

## HSK 7–9

Primary Objective

Develop advanced communication comparable to highly proficient Chinese users.

Typical scenarios include:

- Academic research.
- Professional leadership.
- Debate.
- Public policy.
- Economics.
- Technology.
- Media.
- Literature.
- Cultural analysis.

Learners should communicate naturally in demanding environments.

# Vocabulary Mapping

Every vocabulary item includes:

- HanGo lesson reference.
- Scenario reference.
- HSK level.
- Frequency.
- Mastery status.

Vocabulary may appear before its official HSK level if it is essential for communication within a scenario.

Likewise, some HSK vocabulary may be introduced later if it is not immediately useful in real-life communication.

---

# Grammar Mapping

Every grammar point includes:

- HanGo lesson.
- Scenario.
- HSK reference.
- Difficulty.
- Related grammar.
- Recommended review lessons.

Grammar progression should remain compatible with HSK while prioritizing communication needs.

---

# Competency Mapping

Every completed lesson contributes to both:

- HanGo Competency Framework.
- Estimated HSK readiness.

This allows learners to understand both their communication ability and their approximate HSK preparation level.

# AI Teacher Responsibilities

The AI Teacher should understand:

- The learner's earned General Level (0-2000 points) and its tier name
  (not an HSK number — see `points_system.md`'s 2026-08-24 note) — the
  authoritative signal once it exists, since it is earned through completed
  scenarios rather than declared at onboarding. See `points_system.md`'s
  "AI Teacher Responsibilities" section for the exact precedence rule.
- The learner's self-reported level, used only until real evidence exists.
- Grammar already mastered.
- Vocabulary already mastered.
- Competencies still developing.

The AI should never introduce content significantly above the learner's current level unless explicitly requested.

---

# HSK Preparation Mode

HanGo may include an optional study mode for learners preparing for the official HSK examination.

This mode emphasizes:

- Vocabulary review.
- Grammar review.
- Reading practice.
- Listening practice.
- Mock examinations.
- Time management strategies.

The communication-based curriculum remains the primary learning pathway.

---

# Curriculum Expansion

As HanGo grows, additional content may be mapped to:

- Business Chinese.
- Academic Chinese.
- Industry-specific Chinese.
- Regional vocabulary.
- Chinese proficiency frameworks beyond HSK.

The communication-first philosophy should always remain unchanged.

---

# Success Definition

The HSK Mapping succeeds when learners naturally develop real communication skills while progressing through the HSK proficiency framework without treating HSK as the sole objective of learning Chinese.
