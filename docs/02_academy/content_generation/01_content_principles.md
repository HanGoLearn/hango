# HanGo Content Principles

Version: 1.0

Status: Official

Last Updated: August 2026

---

# Purpose

This document defines the principles that govern every piece of educational content created for HanGo.

Whether content is written by educators or generated by AI, it must follow these principles to ensure a consistent learning experience.

---

# Core Principle

Every piece of content must improve the learner's ability to communicate in Chinese.

Content exists to develop competence, not simply to transfer information.

---

# Principle 1: Communication Before Information

Content should always answer:

"What will the learner be able to do after completing this lesson?"

Instead of:

"Learn 15 new words."

The objective becomes:

"Ask for recommendations at a restaurant."

---

# Principle 2: Context Before Explanation

Learners should first understand the situation.

Only afterward should vocabulary and grammar be explained.

Context creates purpose.

Purpose improves retention.

---

# Principle 3: Every Lesson Solves One Problem

Each lesson should focus on a single communication objective.

Examples:

- Order a meal.
- Introduce yourself professionally.
- Explain a medical symptom.
- Negotiate a meeting time.

Avoid combining unrelated objectives into the same lesson.

---

# Principle 4: Vocabulary Is Functional

Vocabulary should be selected because learners need it to complete a task.

Words should never be included only because they belong to an HSK list.

Every word should answer:

"Why does the learner need this?"

---

# Principle 5: Grammar Supports Communication

Grammar is introduced only when it helps learners express ideas more effectively.

Grammar is a communication tool, not the destination.

---

# Principle 6: Reuse Before Expansion

Previously learned vocabulary and grammar should appear regularly.

Every new lesson should combine:

- Familiar language.
- New language.

This creates long-term retention.

---

# Principle 7: Difficulty Increases Gradually

Each lesson should introduce only a manageable amount of new material.

The learner should feel challenged but not overwhelmed.

---

# Principle 8: Active Production Is Mandatory

Every lesson must require learners to produce Chinese.

Possible activities include:

- Speaking.
- Writing.
- AI conversation.
- Role-play.

Recognition alone is insufficient.

---

# Principle 9: Authenticity

Content should reflect how native speakers actually communicate.

Artificial textbook language should be avoided.

Natural expressions should be prioritized whenever appropriate.

---

# Principle 10: Cultural Relevance

When culture affects communication, it should be explained.

Examples:

- Politeness.
- Greetings.
- Dining etiquette.
- Professional communication.

Language and culture should be taught together.

---

# Principle 11: Personalization

The AI Teacher may personalize:

- Examples.
- Practice exercises.
- Explanations.
- Review activities.

However, the core learning objectives remain consistent for all learners.

---

# Principle 12: Every Lesson Ends With Action

Learners should leave every lesson having completed a meaningful communication task.

Knowledge becomes competence through action.

---

# Success Definition

Content succeeds when learners finish a lesson more capable of communicating in Chinese than when they started.
