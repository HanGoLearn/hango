# HanGo Curriculum Structure

Version: 1.1

Status: Official

Last Updated: August 2026

The Level and Domain examples below were written before the real
curriculum existed and described a different, illustrative structure. This
revision replaces them with the actual 3-chapter / 9-domain structure the
app ships today — see [`points_system.md`](points_system.md) for how it
maps to General Level points and HSK.

---

# Purpose

This document defines how HanGo organizes Chinese learning content from intermediate to advanced proficiency.

The curriculum structure ensures learners follow a clear progression while developing real communication skills through meaningful situations.

---

# Curriculum Philosophy

HanGo combines two complementary structures:

1. Proficiency progression.

2. Real-world communication scenarios.

The curriculum follows a structured path similar to HSK progression while teaching through practical situations.

---

# Curriculum Framework

The HanGo curriculum is organized into five levels:

```
Level

↓

Domain

↓

Scenario

↓

Lesson

↓

Activity
```

---

# 1. Level

A Level represents the learner's overall proficiency stage.

HanGo covers:

## Beginner

Approximately equivalent to:

HSK 1 - HSK 2

Focus:

- Absolute-beginner survival Chinese.
- Greetings, family, daily routines.
- Building the first few hundred words and simplest sentence patterns.

Added 2026-08-09 as the Foundations domain — the curriculum's entry point
for a learner starting from zero.

---

## Intermediate

Approximately equivalent to:

HSK 3 - HSK 4

Focus:

- Daily communication.
- Common situations.
- Building fluency.
- Expanding vocabulary.

---

## Upper Intermediate

Approximately equivalent to:

HSK 5 - HSK 6

Focus:

- Complex conversations.
- Opinions.
- Professional communication.
- Abstract topics.

---

## Advanced

Approximately equivalent to:

HSK 7 - HSK 9

Focus:

- Academic communication.
- Professional language.
- Cultural understanding.
- Nuanced expression.

---

# 2. Domain

A Domain represents a broad area of communication. HanGo's real curriculum
has 9 Domains, organized behind the scenes — the learner never sees this
list directly. What the learner sees instead is 3 **chapters** on the
Learning Journey screen, grouped by difficulty tier rather than by Domain:

| Chapter (learner-facing) | Domains behind it |
|---|---|
| Foundations | Foundations |
| Everyday Life | Daily Life, Travel, Getting Around, Making Friends, Health, Work & Office, Everyday Life |
| Advanced Situations | Advanced Situations |

Examples of what each Domain covers:

## Foundations

Includes:

- Greetings and introductions.
- Family and daily routines.
- Basic shopping, phone calls, school.

---

## Daily Life / Travel / Getting Around / Making Friends / Health / Work & Office

Includes:

- Restaurant, shopping, hotels.
- Public transport, directions.
- Meeting people, small talk.
- Pharmacy and doctor visits.
- Basic workplace communication.

---

## Everyday Life

Includes:

- Grocery shopping, coffee shops, gyms.
- Renting an apartment, roommate issues.
- Online shopping problems, job interviews.

---

## Advanced Situations

Includes:

- Negotiating contracts, resolving workplace conflict.
- Academic seminars, media interviews.
- Legal/notarial procedures, diplomatic negotiation.
- Formal speeches, specialist medical consultations.
- Tech/finance expert panels.

---

# 3. Scenario

A Scenario represents a specific real-life situation.

Examples:

Domain:

Travel

Scenario:

Hotel Check-in

Communication objectives:

The learner can:

- Make a reservation.
- Provide personal information.
- Ask about facilities.
- Solve common problems.

---

# Scenario Structure

Each scenario contains:

```
Scenario

↓

Difficulty Level

↓

Learning Objectives

↓

Lessons

↓

Assessment
```

---

# Difficulty Levels Inside Scenarios

Every scenario can appear at different complexity levels.

Example:

## Restaurant - Intermediate

The learner can:

- Order food.
- Ask prices.
- Request modifications.

---

## Restaurant - Upper Intermediate

The learner can:

- Explain preferences.
- Discuss ingredients.
- Make recommendations.

---

## Restaurant - Advanced

The learner can:

- Discuss cultural differences.
- Analyze food trends.
- Debate social topics.

---

# 4. Lesson

A Lesson represents a focused learning unit.

Every lesson has:

## Communication Goal

What the learner can do after completing it.

Example:

"Order a meal and explain dietary restrictions."

---

## Language Objectives

The language required.

Includes:

- Vocabulary.
- Grammar.
- Expressions.

---

## Practice Objectives

How the learner demonstrates ability.

Includes:

- Speaking.
- Writing.
- Listening.

---

# 5. Activity

Activities are individual learning interactions.

Examples:

- Vocabulary practice.
- Grammar exercises.
- Dialogue analysis.
- Listening questions.
- Speaking tasks.
- Writing tasks.
- AI conversations.

---

# Content Progression Rules

The curriculum follows these rules:

---

## Rule 1: Communication First

Content must support a communication objective.

---

## Rule 2: Controlled Complexity

New concepts are introduced gradually.

---

## Rule 3: Continuous Recycling

Previously learned content appears again in future contexts.

---

## Rule 4: Increasing Independence

Learners receive less support as proficiency increases.

---

# Relationship With HSK

HSK provides:

- Vocabulary guidance.
- Grammar references.
- Level expectations.

However, HanGo reorganizes HSK content into communication-based learning paths.

The learner does not study:

"HSK vocabulary unit 15."

The learner studies:

"How to discuss your career goals."
