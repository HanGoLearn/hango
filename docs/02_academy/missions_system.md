# HanGo Mission System

Version: 1.0

Status: Official

Last Updated: August 2026

---

# Purpose

The Mission System defines the final practical challenge of every HanGo lesson.

A mission represents the learner's opportunity to apply everything learned in a realistic communication task.

Unlike traditional exercises, missions focus on accomplishing an objective rather than answering questions correctly.

---

# Educational Philosophy

Learning is only complete when knowledge is successfully applied.

Every lesson should conclude with a meaningful communication objective that requires the learner to integrate vocabulary, grammar, listening, speaking, and writing skills.

A mission answers one question:

"Can the learner successfully communicate in this situation?"

---

# Mission Flow

Every mission follows the same structure.

```
Mission Brief

↓

Objective

↓

Preparation

↓

Interaction

↓

Challenge

↓

Resolution

↓

Evaluation

↓

Reflection
```

---

# Mission Brief

The learner receives a clear description of the situation.

The brief should explain:

- Where the interaction takes place.
- Who is involved.
- Why the interaction matters.
- What must be accomplished.

The learner should immediately understand the communication goal.

---

# Mission Objectives

Every mission contains one primary objective.

Examples:

Restaurant

Successfully order a complete meal while asking about ingredients.

---

Hotel

Request a room change because of excessive noise.

---

Airport

Ask for assistance after losing your luggage.

---

Business

Present a proposal to a potential client.

---

University

Explain your research topic to your professor.

The objective should always be practical and measurable.

---

# Mission Components

Every mission includes:

- Scenario.
- Context.
- Objective.
- Participants.
- Required vocabulary.
- Required grammar.
- Suggested communication strategies.
- Difficulty level.
- Estimated duration.

---

# Communication Focus

Success is measured by communication effectiveness rather than grammatical perfection.

Learners should be encouraged to solve problems naturally.

Minor mistakes should not prevent mission success if communication remains clear.

---

# Difficulty Levels

Every mission is available at multiple difficulty levels.

---

## Level 1

Guided Mission

The learner receives prompts and hints throughout the interaction.

Ideal for first-time exposure.

---

## Level 2

Supported Mission

The learner receives limited hints.

The conversation becomes more flexible.

---

## Level 3

Independent Mission

No hints are provided.

The learner independently manages the conversation.

---

## Level 4

Adaptive Mission

The AI introduces unexpected situations requiring problem-solving.

---

## Level 5

Expert Mission

The learner must communicate confidently in a dynamic, realistic conversation with multiple objectives.

# AI Dynamic Behavior

The AI controls all mission participants.

The AI should:

- Respond naturally.
- Ask follow-up questions.
- Adapt to learner responses.
- Introduce realistic variations.
- Maintain the objectives of the scenario.

No two missions should feel completely identical.

---

# Unexpected Events

Depending on the selected difficulty, missions may include unexpected situations.

Examples:

Restaurant

- The dish is unavailable.
- The waiter misunderstood the order.
- An allergy must be explained.

Hotel

- The room reservation cannot be found.
- The room is unavailable.
- The payment method fails.

Business

- The client changes the requirements.
- A deadline is moved forward.
- A disagreement occurs.

Unexpected events encourage flexible communication.

---

# Evaluation Criteria

Every mission evaluates:

- Objective completion.
- Vocabulary usage.
- Grammar usage.
- Listening comprehension.
- Speaking fluency.
- Pronunciation.
- Communication effectiveness.
- Confidence.
- Adaptability.

---

# Mission Results

After completion, learners receive:

- Mission status.
- Communication score.
- Strengths.
- Areas for improvement.
- AI recommendations.
- Suggested review lessons.
- Suggested simulations.

Feedback should always emphasize growth.

---

# Mission Retry

Learners may repeat missions at any time.

Each attempt should generate:

- Different dialogue paths.
- Different AI responses.
- Different follow-up questions.
- Different unexpected events.

Replayability is a core design principle.

---

# AI Teacher Responsibilities

During missions, the AI Teacher should:

- Remain in character.
- Avoid breaking immersion.
- Evaluate communication naturally.
- Delay non-critical corrections until the mission ends.
- Generate personalized feedback afterward.

---

# Relationship With Other Systems

The Mission System integrates directly with:

- Lesson Design.
- Scenario System.
- Simulation Engine.
- Competency Framework.
- Assessment System.
- Learning Journey.

Mission results contribute to the learner's long-term competency profile.

---

# Success Definition

The Mission System succeeds when learners confidently transfer classroom knowledge into realistic communication and feel capable of handling similar situations outside HanGo.
