# Purpose

This document defines how dialogues are designed, structured, and integrated into the HanGo learning experience.

Dialogues are the primary vehicle through which learners observe, understand, and practice authentic Chinese communication.

Every dialogue should prepare learners for real conversations outside the platform.

---

# Dialogue Philosophy

People do not communicate using isolated words or grammar rules.

They communicate through conversations.

Therefore, every dialogue should represent a realistic interaction with a clear communication objective.

Dialogues are not reading exercises.

They are communication models.

---

# Educational Objectives

Every dialogue should help learners:

- Understand spoken Chinese.
- Observe vocabulary in context.
- Observe grammar in context.
- Learn natural sentence patterns.
- Identify cultural behaviors.
- Prepare for speaking and writing activities.

---

# Dialogue Structure

Every dialogue contains:

- Title.
- Communication objective.
- Scenario.
- Difficulty level.
- Participants.
- Context description.
- Complete transcript.
- Native audio.
- Slow audio.
- Translation.
- Pinyin toggle.

# Dialogue Progression

Dialogue complexity increases through:

- Conversation length.
- Speaking speed.
- Vocabulary richness.
- Grammar complexity.
- Number of participants.
- Topic abstraction.

Each increase should feel gradual and natural.

---

# Authenticity

Dialogues should reflect real spoken Chinese.

They should include:

- Natural expressions.
- Common sentence patterns.
- Everyday vocabulary.
- Appropriate politeness.
- Realistic reactions.

Artificial textbook conversations should be avoided.

---

# Cultural Context

When appropriate, dialogues should explain:

- Cultural expectations.
- Social etiquette.
- Formality levels.
- Regional differences.
- Pragmatic meaning.

Language should always be presented within its cultural context.

# Reading Support

Learners may choose:

- Characters only.
- Characters + Pinyin.
- Translation.
- Hide translation.
- Hide Pinyin.

Support should gradually decrease as proficiency increases.

---

# Shadowing

Learners repeat after native speakers.

Objectives:

- Improve pronunciation.
- Improve rhythm.
- Improve intonation.
- Improve fluency.

The AI Teacher provides pronunciation feedback.

---

# Role Play

The learner replaces one participant.

Example:

Dialogue:

Customer ↔ Waiter

The learner becomes the customer.

The AI becomes the waiter.

This bridges passive understanding and active communication.

---

# AI Conversation Extension

After completing the dialogue, the AI Teacher continues the conversation naturally.

Examples:

Restaurant:

"What would you like for dessert?"

Hotel:

"Would you like a late check-out?"

Business:

"Can you explain your proposal in more detail?"

The learner applies the same communication skills in a new situation.

---

# Dialogue Evaluation

The system evaluates:

- Listening comprehension.
- Vocabulary recognition.
- Grammar recognition.
- Pronunciation.
- Speaking fluency.
- Communication effectiveness.

---

# Dialogue Reuse

Completed dialogues should continue appearing in future lessons.

Reinforcement methods include:

- Review sessions.
- Speaking Lab.
- Listening practice.
- Simulations.
- AI conversations.

Learners should revisit dialogues with increasing levels of difficulty.

---

# AI Teacher Responsibilities

The AI Teacher should:

- Explain difficult sentences.
- Clarify vocabulary.
- Explain grammar usage.
- Answer learner questions.
- Generate similar dialogues.
- Continue conversations naturally.

---

# Success Definition

The Dialogue System succeeds when learners can understand authentic conversations, participate confidently, and transfer those communication patterns to real-life interactions.
